# Audience, message, and campaign organization

Use before new briefs, campaigns, scripts, storyboards, substantial message
revisions, and audience/publication-readiness reviews. This is a planning workflow
across all formats and visual modes, not a rendering mode. Do not restart strategy
for an isolated technical repair unless the user asks for it.

## Start from a viewer, not an inventory of features

Read the supplied brief, current scripts, evidence, and prior approvals. Distinguish
what is explicitly requested from an inferred recommendation. A technically correct
tour can still fail to explain why a particular viewer should care.

Segment first by role, situation, need, and familiarity with the subject. Add
language, geography, accessibility, or demographic constraints when supplied or
relevant to comprehension. Do not invent ages, purchasing power, cultural traits,
attention-span statistics, or conversion forecasts. Unknown audience preferences
remain assumptions to test, not research findings.

Identify the viewer separately from the buyer, product user, narrator, and on-screen
protagonist. A traveler can anchor a film addressed to event organizers; the story
must then resolve the organizer's problem. Multiple actors do not require a split
if their actions serve one audience's question. An explicitly commissioned broad
overview may serve a shared audience need; explain that common need rather than
forcing a narrow sales target onto it.

## Choose the communication job

Choose a dominant purpose so depth, order, and ending agree. These are useful
distinctions, not mandatory campaign buckets:

| Purpose | Viewer question | Useful ending |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Discovery / promotion | Why is this relevant to me? | One credible benefit and a low-friction next step. |
| Evaluation / trust | Does this fit my needs, costs, or concerns? | Evidence, material limitations, and a relevant evaluation step. |
| Onboarding / tutorial | How do I complete my task? | A recognizable task result and what to do if blocked. |
| Internal operations / training | What am I responsible for? | Role-specific actions, permissions, and exception handling. |
| General editorial education | How or why does this work? | A clear mental model or answered question; no sales CTA required. |

Do not put employee review procedures in a customer acquisition film simply because
they establish product correctness. Do not turn an explicitly requested walkthrough
into a promotion. A film may explain a mechanism while teaching a task, provided
the supporting explanation helps the same viewer complete that task.

## Produce a compact audience/message brief

Reuse the existing production contract rather than creating a competing document.
For a simple request, a short paragraph or table is sufficient. Record:

- **Primary viewer and situation:** who is watching, what brought them here, and
  what they already know. Distinguish known inputs from assumptions.
- **Purpose and placement:** communication job, channel/viewing context, and
  whether this is an introduction, evaluation, or post-adoption resource.
- **Need and friction:** the concrete question, task, or concern being addressed.
- **Promise and proof:** one useful outcome, the available supporting evidence,
  and limits that materially affect the viewer's decision.
- **Message spine:** opening relevance → necessary explanation or actions →
  evidence/result → resolution. Adapt the order to the story, not a rigid template.
- **Ending:** one primary action the viewer can actually take, or an explicit
  learning resolution when a CTA is inappropriate. Verify product permissions and
  the destination before presenting an action as available; do not invent a URL.
- **Exclusions and constraints:** material outside this viewer's task, delivery
  requirements, and the status of audience/message approval.

Summarize it as: “For [viewer in situation], this film explains [question or task]
so they can [outcome], supported by [evidence]. It ends with [action or insight].”

When the audience is unambiguous, state it and proceed within authorized scope.
When two plausible audiences require materially different scripts, recommend one
with reasons and ask the smallest necessary question. Continue reversible outline
work, but do not lock a script or spend on narration using an unresolved choice.
Use the user's existing approval when it covers this brief; do not demand a new
gate number or repeated approval for unchanged decisions.

## Organize a campaign before producing it

For multiple films, propose an inventory with these columns:

`Film | Primary viewer | Purpose | Viewer question / takeaway | Proof | Ending | Keep / refocus / split / merge / defer`

Assign each film a distinct job and explain dependencies or viewing order only
where needed. A social introduction must not assume the viewer watched an internal
tutorial. Shared assets can be reused without sharing every message or CTA.

- **Keep** when one viewer need is already clear.
- **Refocus** when mixed details can be removed without changing the main job.
- **Split** when viewers need different instructions, permissions, or endings.
- **Merge** when two films repeat the same question and intended next step.
- **Defer** when there is no evidenced useful outcome or prerequisite proof.

These are proposals, not authority to change a commissioned count, budget, scope,
or approved script. Preserve requested deliverables until the user accepts a change.
Do not manufacture one promotional film per feature to preserve an initial count.

## Translate the brief into narration and scenes

Make relevance apparent in the opening beat through the viewer's situation,
question, or stakes; saying their role aloud is optional. Avoid an abstract feature
name followed by unexplained system terminology. Do not claim a fixed number of
seconds guarantees attention or retention.

For each scene, identify the viewer's next question, the narrated answer, what
visibly changes to explain it, supporting evidence, and the link to the next beat.
Use the approved script/beat map for this; avoid a second timing source of truth.
Remove or move a scene if its only justification is “the feature exists.”

Prefer consequences the viewer can understand over internal implementation detail.
For example, “set how many rooms you offer for these nights” may serve a hotel
manager better than a discussion of database locks. Technical detail remains
appropriate for an engineering audience or an explicit technical commission.
Keep important limitations, but distinguish public decision-relevant boundaries
from internal QA notes. Evidence labels and safety caveats are not the narrative.

Keep the addressee stable across role handoffs. A supporting actor's action should
answer the primary viewer's question, not suddenly instruct that actor to operate
an interface. Use natural language at the viewer's knowledge level; define a needed
term once, and avoid replacing precision with vague benefit claims.

## Audience/message acceptance

Review the script before paid narration and revisit this check on the encoded cut
when publication readiness is in scope. Report this separately from factual,
visual, and technical checks:

1. Can the intended viewer recognize their situation from the opening itself,
   without needing the internal title or brief?
2. Do the examples, terminology, depth, and sequence match their prior knowledge?
3. Does each scene advance their question, with role changes explained rather
   than switching addressees unnoticed?
4. Does the ending fulfill the opening promise, with a permitted, supported next
   step or a clear learning resolution?
5. Are campaign films distinct enough to justify their separate jobs?

Record concrete script lines or scene/time references for findings and describe
the smallest correction. If only an outline was reviewed, say so. Passing codec,
loudness, or visual checks is not a messaging pass. An agent's instruction review
is not a real audience test; mark actual viewer comprehension and retention as
untested unless such evidence was collected. For a narrow repair, report an
unrelated message problem without silently expanding into script regeneration.
