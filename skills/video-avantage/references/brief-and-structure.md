# Brief and story structure

## Confirm before generating anything

| Field | Example |
| --- | --- |
| The feature, benefit, or differentiator | transparent commission versus a broker |
| The concrete numbers or claims, if any | a disclosed base percentage, a split above the base |
| Script and structure | approved by the requester before any generation |
| Music | a rights-cleared instrumental, or none if the requester will score it |

Push back when a field is missing. Character clips are the expensive part of
this format; a script revision costs nothing by comparison.

## The structure that works: cold open, not slow build

Not chronological setup and payoff. Instead:

1. **Cold open on the payoff**, two to five seconds. State the result as boldly
   as possible, as the very first frame, narrated urgently.
2. **Hard cut and a one-word pattern interrupt**, under 1.5 seconds, for
   example "How?".
3. **Rewind cue**, for example "Three weeks earlier", signalling that the
   mechanism is about to be explained.
4. **The story plays forward**: character scenes alternating with explainer and
   comparison cards.
5. **The payoff repeats as a verbal callback**, restating the result. It is
   earned the second time because the story explained the mechanism.
6. **End card** with the call to action and the contact bar.

The first three seconds decide whether anyone keeps watching, and "here is the
surprising result" stops a scroll better than "let me set the scene".

## Every beat carries a voice

No silent segments. A reaction shot gets a short narrator line rather than
being left bare. Silence in a short vertical video reads as a broken file, not
as a considered pause.

## Character shots

Generate character clips as reaction performances wherever the line might
change in a later revision. A clip whose mouth was animated to specific words
cannot be reused under a different line; see
[narration-and-voice.md](narration-and-voice.md).
