# The Slot Machine (the honest version)

A reward that arrives the same way every time stops registering. The first
badge is exciting, the tenth is furniture. Rewards that vary in timing and
size hold attention far longer; this is B. F. Skinner's variable-ratio finding,
and it is the mechanism behind slot machines and endless feeds.

This skill keeps the insight and drops the casino. The aim is that opening the
app is worth it because something real and new may be there. The aim is not to
keep someone pulling a lever.

## What to produce

1. **The real reward.** What users actually come for, in their words (finding
   a place, getting an enquiry, learning something, a reply). Not points.
2. **Predictability check.** What a returning user sees on the second, fifth
   and twentieth visit. If it is identical, the reward is dead.
3. **Sources of real variation.** What truly changes in the product between
   visits: new items, changes to items they care about, activity from other
   people, progress on something they started.
4. **Variation plan.** Which surfaces show that change, how often it can
   honestly differ, and what the screen shows when nothing changed.

## How to work

- Open the app as a returning user several times and note what differs. Check
  the queries behind the home and results screens: fixed sort orders and
  static sections are the usual cause of sameness.
- Lead with what is new or changed for this person since their last visit.
- Vary the form as well as the content: sometimes a new item, sometimes a
  change to a saved one, sometimes a note that someone responded.
- Keep rewards frequent enough to be expected and irregular enough not to be
  scheduled.
- When nothing new exists, say so plainly and offer the next useful step.

## Honest line (this is the adaptation, do not remove it)

- The variation must come from real change. No invented activity, no fake
  "just listed", no artificial countdowns, no made-up viewer counts.
- No randomness around money, prices, fees or access. Nobody should have to
  open the app again to get a better deal.
- No loot boxes, spins, or rewards that exist only to trigger another open.
- If the product helps people make a serious decision (housing, health,
  money), fresh and relevant beats exciting. Success is a faster good
  decision, not more sessions.

## On a Claude Design canvas

Start the canvas with the Artifact tool's Design type and follow that type's own
instructions for mechanics. This skill decides what goes on the artboards:

- **Same screen, three visits:** the home or results screen drawn three times
  as a returning user would see it on different days, each with different real
  content leading. If the three look alike, the design has failed the check.
- **Reward moments:** the two or three forms a reward can take, each as its
  own artboard at real size.
- **Quiet day:** the state shown when nothing changed, with its next step.

Draw every screen inside the app's real layout and design system. Use plausible
real data, and never draw a signal the product cannot truthfully produce.

## Marketplace note (Roogo and similar)

The reward for a seeker is a place worth visiting; for an owner, real interest
in their property. Real variation is plentiful: new listings matching a saved
search, a price change on a saved listing, a fresh photo set, a first enquiry,
view counts that are true. Put "new since your last visit" first. Do not rank
by random order to seem fresh, and do not show urgency that is not real.

## Report format

```
# Slot Machine: <app or flow>
The real reward (per audience)
What a returning user sees today on visit 2, 5, 20
Real sources of variation
Variation plan: surface | what varies | where the data comes from | quiet-day state
Honest-line check: anything proposed that fails it, removed
Top 3 changes, smallest effort first
```
